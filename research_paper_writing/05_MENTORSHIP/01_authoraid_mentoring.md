# AuthorAID Mentoring — Rising Scholars (INASP)

| | |
|:---|:---|
| **Provider** | AuthorAID, now **Rising Scholars** (INASP — International Network for the Advancement of Science & Practice) |
| **URL** | <https://risingscholars.net> |
| **Cost** | **Free** |
| **Eligibility** | Researchers in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) |
| **Format** | One-to-one mentoring by experienced researchers, cohort courses, and a global peer community |

## What it covers

The world's largest free research-mentoring network: matched mentors review drafts, advise on
methods and statistics, help with journal choice, and coach through revision. INASP's project
page describes "free-to-access online courses in research and proposal writing for early-career
researchers in low- and middle-income countries," and Taylor & Francis lists AuthorAID as a
development partner ("a free, pioneering global network that provides support, mentoring,
resources and training for researchers in low and middle income countries").

## Why it is recommended here

The strongest free *human-in-the-loop* option anywhere: a real mentor reads your actual
manuscript. For eligible researchers this should be the first stop of the whole library —
before any paid coach or subscription. The courses (see `../01_COURSES/17`) and mentoring
network compound: take a cohort course, then keep the mentor.

## How to use it with ARES

- Draft with ARES until the manuscript clears the evaluator gates, *then* request mentor review — spend human attention on the best machine-assisted draft
- Mentor feedback on statistics → `skills/03_methodology_architect/statistical_rigor.md`
- Mentor feedback on framing → `skills/01_ideation/gap_analysis_prompt.md`
