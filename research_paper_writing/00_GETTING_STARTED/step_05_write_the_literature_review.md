# Step 5 of 12 — Write the literature review

| | |
|:---|:---|
| **Phase** | 2 · Write the paper (Week 2) |
| **Time** | ≈ 2–3 hours |
| **Deliverable** | A completed synthesis matrix for 8–10 papers, plus two synthesis paragraphs |
| **Before you start** | [Step 4](step_04_find_your_research_question.md) complete (the matrix needs a question to organize by) |

> **The one idea of this step:** a literature review is an *argument about the state of
> knowledge*, not an annotated list. The tool that forces the difference is the synthesis
> matrix.

## Why this step matters

The beginner failure mode is the "laundry list": *Smith (2021) did X. Jones (2022) did Y. Lee
(2023) did Z.* — one source per paragraph, no comparison, ending nowhere. Reviewers reject
this as "a summary, not a synthesis" and they are right: nothing in it argues toward a gap.

The fix is mechanical. A **synthesis matrix** (rows = sources, columns = the themes *your
question* cares about) forces comparison into every cell. Paragraphs written off a matrix are
automatically synthetic, because the matrix is organized by *idea*, not by *author*.

## What you will do

1. **Grow your pile** — from the 5 papers of Step 3, expand to **8–10** using the same
   three-pass reading (search deliberately: who cites your pillar papers? What do *they* do
   next?).
2. **Choose the organizing principle** — thematic (by sub-question) suits most journal
   articles; methodological (by technique family) when the method *is* the story;
   chronological only when the field's evolution is itself the argument.
3. **Build the matrix** (Work 5.1 below).
4. **Write two synthesis paragraphs** off the matrix (Work 5.2).

## Your work

### Work 5.1 — Build the synthesis matrix (≈60 min)

Columns are the 3–5 themes your RQ cares about. Example for the practice question (hypothetical
papers shown for craft):

```text
                         | Outcome measure | Delay interval | Population    | Design       |
-------------------------|-----------------|----------------|---------------|--------------|
Okoye & Martins (pill.)  | final exam      | none           | 2nd-yr pharm  | quasi-exp    |
Varga (supporting)       | final exam      | none           | 1st-yr econ   | RCT          |
Danh & Reyes (supporting)| 2-wk quiz       | 2 weeks        | HS biology    | RCT          |
Priya et al. (pill.)     | 3-mo retention  | 3 months       | 1st-yr psych  | lab study    |
...
```

Fill every cell or write "n/r" (not reported — itself a finding). **Read the matrix as the
finding:** the column where entries go sparse or conflict is your gap, in the making. In the
example above: nobody has combined *classroom* delivery with *3-month* retention — exactly the
practice question's niche.

### Work 5.2 — Write two synthesis paragraphs (≈60 min)

Every paragraph follows the **synthesis pattern**:

```text
1. CLAIM about the body of work   "Classroom studies consistently measure retrieval
                                   practice at course end."
2. EVIDENCE from 2–5 sources      "(Okoye 2025; Varga 2024; Danh 2023)"
3. POINT OF AGREEMENT/CONFLICT    "Effects are positive but shrink as class size grows,
                                   except in Danh & Reyes."
4. WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED        "No classroom study has measured retention beyond
                                   two weeks."
5. LINK FORWARD                   "The present study therefore includes a three-month
                                   retention test."
```

Pull phrasings for each move from the Phrasebank's *Referring to sources* and *Discussing
findings* sections — that is exactly what they are for.

### Work 5.3 — Run the ARES mirror (optional, 15 min)

Paste your two paragraphs and run `skills/02_literature_command/synthesis_engine.md`. It will
flag summary-sentences (single-source, no comparison) that slipped through. Fix what it finds.

## Exit test

- [ ] Matrix complete for 8–10 papers, every cell filled or marked n/r
- [ ] Each paragraph cites **2+ sources in comparison** (if a paragraph has one citation, it
      is a list item in disguise — merge it or cut it)
- [ ] Each paragraph ends by pointing at the unresolved, linking forward to your study
