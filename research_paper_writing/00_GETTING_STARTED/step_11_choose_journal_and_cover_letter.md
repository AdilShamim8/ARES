# Step 11 of 12 — Choose the journal and write the cover letter

| | |
|:---|:---|
| **Phase** | 3 · Finish and submit (Week 7) |
| **Time** | ≈ 2 hours |
| **Deliverable** | A three-venue comparison worksheet with a chosen target, and a four-paragraph cover letter |
| **Before you start** | [Step 10](step_10_edit_and_self_review.md) complete — the manuscript is polished |

> **The one idea of this step:** journals reject more often for *fit* than for quality. The
> cover letter is the only place you argue fit — and a generic letter tells the editor you
> submitted everywhere at once.

## Why this step matters

A paper at the wrong venue dies in editorial triage without ever reaching a reviewer — days
or weeks lost, morale lost, sometimes a rushed resubmission elsewhere that is *also* wrong.
Twenty minutes of deliberate comparison changes those odds. And the cover letter is your one
unfiltered sentence to the editor: it should read like it could not possibly belong to any
other journal.

## Your work

### Work 11.1 — The venue comparison worksheet (≈60 min)

Shortlist three candidate venues (your Step-3 pillar papers tell you where work like yours
lives). For each, fill a row — and *read one recent published paper per venue* before
judging fit; the abstracts alone mislead:

```text
                       | Venue A      | Venue B      | Venue C
Scope fit (1–5)        |             |             |
Audience              |             |             |
Article limits        |             |             |
Review model & speed  |             |             |
Open-access cost      |             |             |
Recent paper closest  | (cite it)   | (cite it)   | (cite it)
  to mine — how close?|             |             |
```

Pick the winner on **scope fit and audience first**, limits second, speed third. Never pick
primarily on impact factor — a paper that fits is a paper that survives review.

### Work 11.2 — The four-paragraph cover letter (≈45 min)

```text
P1 · SUBMISSION    Title, article type, and the three legal sentences: original,
                   unpublished, not under consideration elsewhere.
P2 · THE PAPER     Problem → finding → significance, in three sentences, written for
                   the journal's BROAD readership (not your subfield).
P3 · FIT           Why THIS journal: name its aims & scope, the audience this serves,
                   optionally the recent paper your work extends.
P4 · LOGISTICS     Corresponding author, conflicts of interest, funding, suggested
                   reviewers (if the venue invites them — choose active authors in the
                   area, never collaborators or friends), excluded reviewers with reason.
```

**Never:** flattery ("your prestigious journal"), impact-factor talk, or pre-emptive
defensiveness about limitations. One page, no more.

**Practice example, paragraph 3 (hypothetical):**

> *This work extends the classroom-retrieval-practice line your journal has developed
> recently (e.g., your 2025 report on spaced quizzing in pharmacy education) by adding the
> delay-measurement dimension that line currently lacks. It will interest your readers in
> science education, cognitive psychology, and instructional design alike.*

That paragraph could belong to exactly one journal. That is the standard.

## Exit test

- [ ] Worksheet complete for three venues, each with a recent-paper fit citation
- [ ] Venue chosen on scope/audience grounds, stated in one sentence you can defend
- [ ] Cover letter's P3 names the journal's own scope and could match no other journal
