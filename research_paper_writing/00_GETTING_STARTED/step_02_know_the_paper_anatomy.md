# Step 2 of 12 — Know the paper's anatomy

| | |
|:---|:---|
| **Phase** | 1 · Foundations (Week 1) |

| **Deliverable** | One real published paper, annotated: every section labeled, word-counted, and its gap sentence highlighted |

> **The one idea of this step:** you cannot write a structure you have never consciously seen.
> Today you dissect one real paper the way an anatomist would.

## Why this step matters

Reviewers judge structure before they judge science — a paper that misplaces its moves reads
as amateur within one page. The universal skeleton is **AIMRAD**: **A**bstract,
**I**ntroduction, **M**ethods, **R**esults, **A**nd **D**iscussion, plus the surrounding parts
(title, literature review, research questions, conclusion). The 9-part breakdown matches the
nine videos of the anchor playlist — watch the first one now (1 min 48 s):
<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VIy4A3zmljA>.

## What you will do

1. **Watch Ref-n-Write video #1** (1:48) — the aerial view of a research paper. Study notes:
   [`../02_VIDEO_COURSES/video_notes/video_01_course_introduction.md`](../02_VIDEO_COURSES/video_notes/video_01_course_introduction.md)
2. **Pick one real paper** from your field — one of the three you saved in Step 1, ideally one
   close to the kind of study you want to run. It must be a full research article (not a
   review, not a letter).
3. **Label the nine parts** directly in the PDF or a printed copy (use margin notes or a
   highlighting color per part).
4. **Find and highlight the gap sentence** — the exact sentence in the Introduction that says
   what was still unknown. This sentence is the hinge of the whole paper; every later step of
   your own roadmap exists to produce one like it.

## Your work

### Work 2.1 — Label the anatomy (20 min)

Mark these on your chosen paper:

```text
[1] Title          [2] Abstract         [3] Introduction
[4] Literature review (may sit inside the Introduction)
[5] Research question / hypotheses (usually the last paragraph of the Introduction)
[6] Methods        [7] Results          [8] Discussion    [9] Conclusion
```

Note where your paper *deviates* from the template (venues differ). Deviations are fine —
conscious deviation is craft, unconscious deviation is error.

### Work 2.2 — The word-count audit (15 min)

Count the words of each main section (Methods, Results, Introduction, Discussion — approximate
is fine) and fill this ledger:

```text
Section        Words        % of body    What I expected    What surprised me
Introduction   [count]      [pct]        [note]             [note]
Methods        [count]      [pct]        [note]             [note]
Results        [count]      [pct]        [note]             [note]
Discussion     [count]      [pct]        [note]             [note]
```

Typical proportions in journal articles: Methods and Results together outweigh the
Introduction; the Discussion is usually the longest prose section. If your numbers differ,
that is data about your *field's* conventions — write it in the last column.

### Work 2.3 — Copy the gap sentence (5 min)

Copy the paper's gap sentence into your notes, verbatim, with its page number. Underneath it,
write one sentence: what *kind* of gap is it? (unknown mechanism / missing measurement /
conflicting findings / untested method…)

## Exit test

- [ ] All nine parts labeled on a real paper
- [ ] Word-count ledger filled for the four body sections
- [ ] One gap sentence copied, and its type identified
