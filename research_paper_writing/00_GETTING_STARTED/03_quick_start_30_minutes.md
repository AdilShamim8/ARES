# Quick start — your first 30 minutes

If you do nothing else with this library today, do this. Total time: about 30 minutes.

## Minute 0–2 · See the whole paper at once

Watch **Ref-n-Write video #1, "Course Introduction: How to Write a Research paper?"** (1 min 48 s,
≈114K views):
<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VIy4A3zmljA>
Full playlist (9 videos, ≈195,450 total views):
<https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL-W1Hxv8i_Lc5n-uT52HLY2wh8xaiaHzc>
Notes: [`../02_VIDEO_COURSES/video_notes/video_01_course_introduction.md`](../02_VIDEO_COURSES/video_notes/video_01_course_introduction.md)

You now know the nine moving parts of a paper: title, abstract, introduction, literature review,
research questions, methods, results, discussion, conclusion.

## Minute 2–5 · Find the guide for the section you must write

Open [`../07_SECTION_GUIDES/`](../07_SECTION_GUIDES/) and skim the file for the section that is
blocking you. Each guide is one screen long and ends with an ARES command.

## Minute 5–15 · Enroll in one free course (choose one)

| If you are… | Enroll in | Profile |
|:---|:---|:---|

## Minute 15–20 · Install two free tools

1. **Zotero** — free, open-source reference manager (collect → cite → share):
   <https://www.zotero.org> · profile: [`../06_TOOLS/05_zotero.md`](../06_TOOLS/05_zotero.md)
2. **Manchester Academic Phrasebank** — free phrase bank for every rhetorical move in a paper
   (introducing work, describing methods, discussing findings…):
   <https://www.phrasebank.manchester.ac.uk> · profile: [`../04_FREE_GUIDES/03_manchester_academic_phrasebank.md`](../04_FREE_GUIDES/03_manchester_academic_phrasebank.md)

## Minute 20–25 · Put a mentor within reach

Skim [`../05_MENTORSHIP/`](../05_MENTORSHIP/) and bookmark the one that fits you — free
AuthorAID mentoring ( [`../05_MENTORSHIP/01_authoraid_mentoring.md`](../05_MENTORSHIP/01_authoraid_mentoring.md) )
if you are in a low- or middle-income country, or a paid coach otherwise.

## Minute 25–30 · Connect it to ARES

Open the parent repository's [`../README.md`](../README.md), paste `MASTER_PROMPT.md` into your
AI assistant, and run the skill that matches the section you are writing. The library taught you
*what* good looks like; ARES now enforces it on your actual draft.

## What you have after 30 minutes

A mental map of the paper, a section guide in hand, one course enrolled, two tools installed, a
mentor bookmarked, and ARES running — a complete, zero-cost writing stack.
