# Step 7 of 12 — Write the results

| | |
|:---|:---|
| **Phase** | 2 · Write the paper (Weeks 3–4) |
| **Time** | ≈ 2–3 hours |
| **Deliverable** | Three results paragraphs + one figure, each passing its checklist |
| **Before you start** | [Step 6](step_06_write_the_methods.md) complete and your analysis done |

> **The one idea of this step:** Results *report*; they do not argue. Interpretation belongs
> to the Discussion. The Results section's discipline is exactly this separation.

## Why this step matters

Beginner Results sections bleed interpretation into reporting ("this surprising and important
improvement shows spaced repetition is powerful…") — which tells reviewers the author does not
control the genre. Worse: adjectives where numbers belong ("significantly better" — by how
much?). The professional pattern is mechanical, and it is what this step drills.

## What you will do

1. Order results to answer your **research question**, not the chronology of your experiments
   (nobody cares that you ran the retention test second).
2. Write each unit of results as **finding → evidence → pointer**.
3. Build **one figure** that carries the headline finding.
4. Attach **uncertainty to every number**.

## Your work

### Work 7.1 — Three finding paragraphs (≈60 min)

The pattern, sentence by sentence:

```text
FINDING   One plain sentence: what the data shows.
          "Weekly spaced-retrieval quizzes improved final-exam scores."
EVIDENCE  The numbers, with uncertainty, and the comparison basis.
          "Mean 74.9 ± 6.3 vs. 68.1 ± 7.0 (d = 0.98, 95% CI 0.41–1.55; ANCOVA
           adjusting for midterm, p = .003)."
POINTER   Where the reader verifies it.
          "(Fig. 1; Table 2)."
```

A neutral comparison sentence may follow — *relation* only, never *meaning* ("scores differed
across all four course modules", not "this proves the quizzes worked").

**Practice example (hypothetical):** the block above is a complete Results paragraph — four
sentences, zero adjectives doing a number's job, every figure claim verifiable.

### Work 7.2 — One figure, built to checklist (≈60 min)

Make the single figure that answers your RQ at a glance, then audit it:

- [ ] **One message** — if it makes two points, it is two figures
- [ ] **Self-contained caption** — what, where, n, and the key reading ("Arrow marks the
      3-month retention difference")
- [ ] **Legible at print size** — axis labels with units; fonts ≥ 7 pt at final size
- [ ] **Colorblind-safe** — never encode meaning in color alone (add shape/linestyle)
- [ ] **Referenced from the text** — "(Fig. 1)" appears in a Results paragraph

### Work 7.3 — The uncertainty sweep (≈15 min)

Search your draft for every bare number. Attach, or note why not attachable, one of: ± spread
(SD/CI), n, or the test statistic. "74.9" alone is a rumor; "74.9 ± 6.3, n = 32" is a result.

## Exit test

- [ ] Three paragraphs, each following finding → evidence → pointer
- [ ] One figure passing all five checklist points
- [ ] Zero bare numbers in the Results section
- [ ] Zero interpretation words (*proves, demonstrates, shows that X is important*) in Results
