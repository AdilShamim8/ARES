# Step 10 of 12 — Edit and self-review

| | |
|:---|:---|
| **Phase** | 3 · Finish and submit (Week 6) |
| **Time** | ≈ 3 hours |
| **Deliverable** | A manuscript ≥10% shorter than its first complete draft, passing the read-aloud test and (optional) the ARES Clarity gate at ≥80 |
| **Before you start** | [Step 9](step_09_write_conclusion_abstract_title.md) complete — you have a full draft |

> **The one idea of this step:** first drafts are for *you*; revisions are for *readers*.
> Editing is not writing done again — it is a different act, done in passes, one kind of
> problem at a time.

## Why this step matters

Nobody writes a clean first draft — senior researchers least of all; they write fast and
revise ruthlessly, because they know the reader experiences only the last pass. The
beginner's mistake is mixing all editing into one vague "make it better" sweep, which fixes
nothing systematically. The professional method is **three separate passes**, each hunting
one class of problem.

## Your work

### Work 10.1 — Pass 1 · Structure (≈45 min)

Do not touch sentences. Read only the *skeleton*:

- Does each section do only its own job? (Results clean of interpretation, Discussion clean
  of new results — Steps 7–8 enforced this; verify it survived revision.)
- Section lengths vs. the Step-2 word ledger of your model paper — anything wildly off?
- Does the Introduction funnel actually end in your gap statement?
- Do paragraphs have exactly one job each? Merge or split where they don't.
- Read **only the first sentence of every paragraph**, in order. That chain should read as a
  coherent outline of the paper. Where the chain breaks, the structure breaks.

### Work 10.2 — Pass 2 · Clarity (≈75 min)

Now sentence level. Three hunts, in order of yield:

**Hunt A — Clutter.** Delete words that do no work. Classic first kills:
"*in order to* → to", "*due to the fact that* → because", "*it should be noted that* → (delete)",
"*a total of 12* → 12", "*very* / *quite* / *really* → (delete)".

**Hunt B — Verbs.** Convert nominalizations back into verbs: "*made a comparison of* →
compared", "*conducted an investigation into* → investigated". Prefer active voice where the
actor matters ("We randomized" beats "Randomization was performed").

**Hunt C — Hedging precision.** Sort every hedge into *earned* ("suggests, within this
cohort") or *unearned* ("it could perhaps be suggested that…"). Earned stays, unearned is
either deleted or upgraded to a plain claim.

Record your word count **before and after** this pass. A serious clarity pass removes ≥10% of
the words while losing no content — that number is your proof the pass happened.

### Work 10.3 — Pass 3 · Surface + read-aloud + gates (≈45 min)

- **Read the whole paper aloud.** Every stumble marks a sentence a reader will stumble on;
  fix each one before moving on. (This test is brutally effective and costs 30 minutes.)
- Surface sweep: notation consistency, abbreviation definitions at first use, table/figure
  numbering, citation format uniformity (Zotero handles this if you cited from it).
- **(With ARES)** run `evaluators/CLARITY_ANALYZER.md` on the full draft — target ≥80. Also
  run `evaluators/RIGOR_RUBRIC.md` if the paper carries empirical claims (target ≥75). Treat
  any sub-gate as a to-do list, not a verdict.

## Exit test

- [ ] Pass 1 done: paragraph-first-sentence chain reads as an outline
- [ ] Pass 2 done: word count reduced ≥10% with no content lost
- [ ] Read-aloud done: every stumble fixed
- [ ] (If used) Clarity ≥80; every flagged issue resolved or consciously accepted
