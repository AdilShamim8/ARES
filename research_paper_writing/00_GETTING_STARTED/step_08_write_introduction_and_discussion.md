# Step 8 of 12 — Introduction and Discussion

| | |
|:---|:---|
| **Phase** | 2 · Write the paper (Weeks 4–5) |
| **Time** | ≈ 3 hours |
| **Deliverable** | A complete Introduction (built on your Step-4 gap statement) and a six-move Discussion |
| **Before you start** | [Step 7](step_07_write_the_results.md) complete |

> **The one idea of this step:** the Introduction decides whether a reviewer *believes there
> is a gap*; the Discussion decides whether your *claims survive reading*. These are the two
> sections where papers are actually won or lost.

## Why this step matters

More papers die in the Introduction than anywhere else — usually by spending 80% of their
length summarizing the field and 20% claiming the contribution, leaving the reviewer unsure
what is new. And more Discussion sections overclaim than underclaim — each unearned "proves"
is a reviewer's easy victory. Both failure modes have mechanical fixes, below.

## Your work

### Work 8.1 — The Introduction funnel, sentence by sentence (≈60 min)

The Introduction is a funnel: broad context → specific problem → the gap → your contribution
→ roadmap. Build on the gap statement you wrote in Step 4:

```text
1. TERRITORY (2–3 sentences)  The field and its stakes. Cite reviews, not textbooks.
2. NICHE (2–4 sentences)      What specifically remains unsolved. THIS is your Step-4
                              gap statement — the paper's hinge.
3. OCCUPATION (3–5 sentences) Your question, approach, and what you found — plainly,
                              with the contribution NAMED.
4. ROADMAP (0–1 sentences)    "Section 2 reviews…; Section 3 presents…" — check two
                              recent papers in your venue; many consider it filler.
```

**The 30% rule:** the literature-summary material (Territory + other people's work inside
Niche) should be at most ~30% of the Introduction. If it runs longer, move the depth into the
literature review proper (Step 5's paragraphs) and let the Introduction point to it.

### Work 8.2 — The Discussion skeleton, six moves (≈75 min)

```text
1. RESTATE      the finding in one sentence — no new numbers.
2. INTERPRET    what it means; the mechanism — why the result came out this way.
3. CONTEXTUALIZE agreement/conflict with the pillars from your Step-3 notes, and WHY
                the difference (population? measure? delay?).
4. LIMITATIONS  honest and specific — and each paired with its consequence:
                "X limits generalization to Y; the effect on Z is therefore tentative."
5. IMPLICATIONS what changes for theory or practice.
6. FUTURE WORK  one or two concrete next studies — pull from your Step-4 spare RQs.
```

The pairing in move 4 is what separates honesty from decoration: a limitation *without* its
consequence reads like ritual; a limitation *with* its consequence reads like judgment.

### Work 8.3 — The overclaim strike-out pass (≈30 min)

Hunt your Discussion for every sentence the data cannot carry, and strike or soften:

```text
STRIKE OR SOFTEN                     REPLACE WITH
"proves" / "demonstrates conclusively"  "is consistent with" / "supports"
"researchers should adopt X"            "in settings like this cohort, X merits trial"
generalization beyond the sample        "within first-year biology courses, …"
causal language in a correlational      associational phrasing, stated plainly
design
```

Run `skills/06_quality_fortress/logic_validator.md` (parent repo) on the Discussion if you
have ARES loaded — it automates exactly this pass. Then fix what it flags.

## Exit test

- [ ] Introduction's Niche block is your Step-4 gap statement, lightly polished
- [ ] Literature material ≤ ~30% of the Introduction
- [ ] Every Discussion limitation is paired with its consequence
- [ ] Zero unearned causal/conclusive language survives the strike-out pass
