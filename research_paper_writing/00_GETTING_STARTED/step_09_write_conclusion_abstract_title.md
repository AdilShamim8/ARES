# Step 9 of 12 — Conclusion, abstract, and title

| | |
|:---|:---|
| **Phase** | 2 · Write the paper (Week 5) |
| **Time** | ≈ 2 hours |
| **Deliverable** | A three-sentence conclusion, a five-move abstract, and a title passing the six-point checklist |
| **Before you start** | [Step 8](step_08_write_introduction_and_discussion.md) complete |

> **The one idea of this step:** these are the most-read parts of the paper, and they are all
> written **last**. The abstract is the paper in miniature; the title is the paper in one
> line; the conclusion closes the loop your research question opened.

## Why this step matters

Reading order in the real world is not Introduction-first: it is **title → abstract →
figures → conclusion**, and only then does a reader decide to invest. Editors triage on the
abstract before anything else. These ~350 words are, per word, the highest-stakes text you
will write.

## Your work

### Work 9.1 — The three-sentence conclusion (≈20 min)

```text
1. ANSWER       "We showed that [X], by [method in six words], achieving [headline result]."
2. SIGNIFICANCE what this changes — for the sub-question, the field, or practice.
3. HORIZON      the next question this opens (your Step-4 spare RQ #2 lives here).
```

Hard rule: **nothing new** enters a conclusion — no new results, no new citations, no new
claims. If a new idea appears, it belonged in the Discussion; move it there now.

### Work 9.2 — The five-move abstract, with word budget (≈50 min)

Total budget: your venue's cap (typically 150–300 words — check `templates/` in the parent
repo; Nature's summary paragraph is 200). Allocate before writing:

```text
MOVE                BUDGET     CRAFT
1. CONTEXT          ~1 sentence   field + stake
2. GAP              ~1 sentence   the niche from your funnel — compressed
3. METHODS          1–2 sentences approach, data, scale
4. RESULTS          2–3 sentences ACTUAL numbers — the finding, not "results are discussed"
5. IMPLICATION      ~1 sentence   what changes now
```

Then apply the abstract's standing rules: no citations; no undefined abbreviations; numbers
over adjectives ("23% faster", never "significantly faster"); it must survive **alone** — no
"as shown in Section 3". The standalone test: hand it to a colleague who reads *only* the
abstract and asks them what the paper found, how, and so what. If they can answer all three,
it passes.

**Practice example (hypothetical, 119 words):**

> *Retrieval practice improves retention across classroom settings, but classroom studies
> rarely measure retention beyond the final exam. We report a two-group classroom experiment
> (N = 64 first-year biology students) testing whether weekly spaced-retrieval quizzes
> improve both final-exam performance and retention three months later. The quiz group
> outperformed restudy controls on the final exam (74.9 vs. 68.1, d = 0.98) and maintained
> the difference at three months (71.2 vs. 63.5, d = 1.02). Gains were consistent across
> course modules. Weekly spaced retrieval appears to support durable, not just immediate,
> learning in introductory science courses; delay-measurement should be standard in
> classroom intervention research.*

Five moves, six sentences, two numbers with effect sizes — count them.

### Work 9.3 — Title and keywords (≈40 min)

Generate **three candidate titles**, then run the checklist on each and keep the winner:

- [ ] Names the **object of study** and the **method or contribution type**
- [ ] Contains the 2–4 terms a searcher would actually query
- [ ] Avoids "Novel", "A Study of", and decoration
- [ ] Fits the venue's length norm (many journals cap ~15 words — check `templates/`)
- [ ] Survives the *alone-in-a-search-result* test — needs no surrounding context
- [ ] Honest: claims exactly what the paper delivers

Keywords: use the field's controlled vocabulary (MeSH, ACM CCS, IEEE thesaurus) where the
venue supports it; do not just repeat title words — cover the synonyms searchers type.

## Exit test

- [ ] Conclusion is ≤ 3 sentences and contains nothing new
- [ ] Abstract passes the standalone test, within the venue's word cap
- [ ] Winning title passes all six checklist points
