# Step 12 of 12 — Submit and survive peer review

|:---|:---|
| **Phase** | 3 · Finish and submit (Week 8) |
| **Time** | ≈ 2 hours, then the waiting period |
| **Deliverable** | A complete submission packet, a simulated reviewer response, and a plan for the waiting months |
| **Before you start** | [Step 11](step_11_choose_journal_and_cover_letter.md) complete — venue chosen, letter written |

> **The one idea of this step:** submission packets fail on mechanics, and peer review is a
> conversation, not a verdict. Both are manageable, and both reward preparation.

## Why this step matters

After the months of work, papers die at submission for embarrassing reasons: a figure at the
wrong resolution, a missing data-availability statement, an ethics box left blank — desk
rejections that never reach a reviewer. And when reviews come back (they will come back with
requests — a *request to revise is a success*, not a failure), the authors who respond
point-by-point, calmly, with evidence, get accepted; the authors who argue everything,
pointlessly, do not.

## Your work

### Work 12.1 — Assemble the packet (≈45 min)

The universal checklist (confirm each against your venue's submission page — names vary):

- [ ] Manuscript file in the venue's template, within every limit (Step 9 checked abstract;
      re-check pages, figures, reference count now)
- [ ] Figures as separate files at required resolution, named in order (fig1.tif, …)
- [ ] Cover letter (Step 11) as a separate upload or text box, as the venue prefers
- [ ] **Data availability statement** — where, how accessed, under what license
- [ ] **Conflict-of-interest declaration** for every author (or a stated absence)
- [ ] **Author contributions** (CRediT-style roles) where the venue offers the field
- [ ] Funding statement, grant numbers included
- [ ] Ethics/approval numbers appearing in the Methods AND in the submission form
- [ ] ORCID for the corresponding author (registered at <https://orcid.org> if not yet)
- [ ] All co-authors have seen and approved the final version — by email, on record

### Work 12.2 — Rehearse the reviewer conversation (≈45 min)

Write a response to one simulated reviewer comment about your own paper. Take the harshest
question your Step-10 self-review raised, and answer it in the reviewer-response format:

```text
REVIEWER:  "The three-month retention test cannot support a durability claim; the
            sample is single-institution."
RESPONSE:  "We agree the retention window limits the strength of the durability claim
            and have softened it accordingly (p. 14, lines 3–8: 'consistent with durable
            learning' rather than 'demonstrates durability'). We now state the
            single-institution scope as a limitation with its consequence (Discussion,
            Limitations, added). A multi-site replication is a natural next step and is
            noted as such."
```

The format is the discipline: **quote the comment → agree or contest with evidence → say
exactly what changed and where (page, line) → never argue tone.** Reviewers spend unpaid
hours on your paper; the response's visible respect is repaid in visible patience.

### Work 12.3 — Plan the waiting period (≈15 min)

Typical first decisions take weeks to months. Decide now, in writing: (a) the **next paper**
you will start — your Step-4 spare RQ #1 deserves its own Step-1-to-12 run; (b) a rule for
checking the submission portal (once a week, not hourly); (c) that a rejection routes the
paper to Worksheet venue #2 with a one-day turnaround, unchanged except for whatever the
reviews taught you.

## Exit test

- [ ] Every packet checklist item confirmed against the venue's own submission page
- [ ] One reviewer response written in the quote → agree/contest → what-changed format
- [ ] The waiting-period plan exists in writing, including the next paper

## What happens after you submit

Triage (editor-only, days) → review (weeks to months) → decision: accept (rare first time),
**minor/major revision** (the usual, and a win — this roadmap trained you for it), or reject
(→ venue #2). On revision invitations: reread every comment twice before answering any,
respond point-by-point in the Work-12.2 format, and resubmit with a summary letter. The
Nature *Focus on Peer Review* course (free) shows this whole cycle from the editor's chair:
[`../01_COURSES/12_nature_focus_on_peer_review.md`](../01_COURSES/12_nature_focus_on_peer_review.md).
