## What this section must accomplish

The cover letter is the only place you argue *fit* rather than *findings*: why this journal,
why now, and why the editors' readers specifically. Editors triage on it — a generic letter
signals a submission blasted to many venues. Wiley's course gives it a four-lesson module,
recognizing it as the component first-time authors most often under-write.

## Working rules

**The four-paragraph cover letter**
1. **Submission statement**: title, article type, and that it is original, unpublished, and not
   under consideration elsewhere
2. **The paper in three sentences**: problem, finding, significance — written for the journal's
   broad readership, not your subfield
3. **Fit**: why *this* journal — cite its aims & scope, name the audience this serves
4. **Logistics**: corresponding author, conflicts of interest, funding, suggested reviewers
   (several venues invite or require this — Nature's *Publishing a Research Paper* module
   teaches how to choose them), and any preferred/excluded reviewers with reasons

**Never**: flattery ("your prestigious journal"), promises about impact factors, or
pre-emptive defensiveness about limitations.

## Session protocol

1. **Watch** the paired Ref-n-Write video (5–10 minutes).
2. **Read** this guide's working rules once.
3. **Draft or revise** your section applying one rule at a time — not all at once.
4. **Run** `skills/07_journal_strategy/cover_letter_engine.md` on the result.
5. **Score** with `evaluators/CLARITY_ANALYZER.md` (target ≥80) and, where the section carries
