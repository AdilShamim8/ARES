## What this section must accomplish

The research question is the paper's load-bearing beam: every other section is an answer,
evidence, or defense of it. A good RQ is focused (one question, not a theme), answerable (the
data and method exist or are collectable), falsifiable (for empirical claims — you can say what
result would refute it), and sized to the venue (one paper's worth). Hypotheses are RQs in
committable form: a directional prediction you can accept or reject.

## Working rules

**From topic to RQ in three moves**
1. Topic → question: add "what/how/why/when" and a measurable object
   ("graph neural networks" → "how does message-passing depth affect over-smoothing in GNNs?")
2. Question → focused question: bind it to system, scale, and population
3. Focused question → hypothesis (empirical work): state the prediction and the test that could
   refute it — this is what `01_ideation/hypothesis_forge.md` automates, including the
   falsifiability stress-test

**Test**: if your Methods section could be written without revealing the RQ's answer, the RQ
is weak. Grad Coach's methodology videos treat this coupling explicitly.

## Session protocol

1. **Watch** the paired Ref-n-Write video (5–10 minutes).
2. **Read** this guide's working rules once.
3. **Draft or revise** your section applying one rule at a time — not all at once.
4. **Run** `skills/01_ideation/hypothesis_forge.md` on the result.
5. **Score** with `evaluators/CLARITY_ANALYZER.md` (target ≥80) and, where the section carries
   empirical claims, `evaluators/RIGOR_RUBRIC.md`.
