## What this section must accomplish

The title is the paper's most-read sentence — it is read in search results, reference lists,
and reviewer triage. A good title is specific, indexable, and honest: it names the system or
phenomenon, the method or approach, and (where meaningful) the outcome. Keywords do the
discovery work that the title cannot: they place the paper in the taxonomy librarians and
databases use. Wiley's dedicated modules treat title and keywords as separate crafts for good
reason — the title persuades humans, keywords persuade machines.

## Working rules

**A six-point checklist for any title**
1. Names the object of study (system, dataset, organism, theory)
2. Names the method or contribution type (review, meta-analysis, framework, benchmark)
3. Contains the 2–4 terms a searcher would actually query
4. Avoids "Novel", "A Study of", and question marks unless the venue loves them
5. Fits the venue's length norm (check the template — many journals cap at ~15 words)
6. Survives the "alone in a search result" test — no context needed

**Keywords**: do not repeat title words one-for-one; use the discipline's controlled
vocabulary (MeSH, ACM CCS, IEEE Thesaurus) where the venue supports it.

## Session protocol

1. **Watch** the paired Ref-n-Write video (5–10 minutes).
2. **Read** this guide's working rules once.
3. **Draft or revise** your section applying one rule at a time — not all at once.

5. **Score** with `evaluators/CLARITY_ANALYZER.md` (target ≥80) and, where the section carries
