## What this section must accomplish

A literature review is an *argument about the state of knowledge*, not an annotated list.
Strong reviews are organized thematically or methodologically — by camps, approaches, or
chronologies of ideas — and each paragraph ends by pointing at what the camp has *not* done,
building the wall your gap statement walks through. The synthesis is what makes it a review:
comparison across sources, not summary of each in turn.

## Working rules

**Organizing principles that work**
- **Thematic**: group by sub-question or approach (most common in journal articles)
- **Methodological**: group by technique family — useful when methods *are* the story
- **Chronological**: only when the field's evolution is itself the argument

**The synthesis paragraph pattern**: claim about a body of work → evidence from 2–5 sources
(cited) → point of agreement or conflict → what remains unresolved → link forward to your
study. Manchester's Phrasebank *Referring to sources* section supplies the sanctioned phrasings.

**Tools**: Zotero collections per theme; SciSpace for corpus search; then
`02_literature_command/citation_network_analysis.md` for the network view of which work is
load-bearing.

## Session protocol

1. **Watch** the paired Ref-n-Write video (5–10 minutes).
2. **Read** this guide's working rules once.
3. **Draft or revise** your section applying one rule at a time — not all at once.
4. **Run** `skills/02_literature_command/synthesis_engine.md` on the result.
5. **Score** with `evaluators/CLARITY_ANALYZER.md` (target ≥80) and, where the section carries
   empirical claims, `evaluators/RIGOR_RUBRIC.md`.
