## What this section must accomplish

Results report; they do not argue. Each unit of results is a finding stated plainly, then the
evidence for it — the number, the figure, the table — with statistics attached. Interpretation
belongs to the Discussion; the Results section's discipline is separation. Structure follows
the logic of the RQs (or the pipeline of the method), not the chronological order in which
experiments were run.

## Working rules

**The results paragraph pattern**
1. **Finding sentence**: what the data shows, in plain words ("Model X reduced error by 23% over the strongest baseline")
2. **Evidence**: the exact numbers with uncertainty ("31.4 ± 1.2 vs. 40.8 ± 2.1 on benchmark Y, n=5 seeds")
3. **Pointer**: where the reader verifies it ("Fig. 3, Table 2")
4. (Optional) one neutral comparison sentence — saved from interpretation, just relation

**Figures/tables**: each earns its space — one message per figure; captions self-contained;
units on every axis. Alley's *Craft of Scientific Writing* has the strongest treatment of
figure-text economy; `04_data_intelligence/visualization_master.md` enforces it in ARES.

## Session protocol

1. **Watch** the paired Ref-n-Write video (5–10 minutes).
2. **Read** this guide's working rules once.
3. **Draft or revise** your section applying one rule at a time — not all at once.
4. **Run** `skills/05_writing_excellence/results_power.md` on the result.
5. **Score** with `evaluators/CLARITY_ANALYZER.md` (target ≥80) and, where the section carries
   empirical claims, `evaluators/RIGOR_RUBRIC.md`.
