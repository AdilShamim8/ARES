## What this section must accomplish

Figures and tables are results in their most-scanned form: most readers' first pass is the
figures, the abstract, and the conclusions — in that order. Each display item must carry one
message, be readable at print size, and be self-contained (caption explains it without the
body text). Wiley devotes a four-lesson module to them; Alley's book treats figure-text economy
as a core craft; ARES's visualization skill enforces the full standard.

## Working rules

**The display-item checklist**
- **One message per item** — if a figure makes two points, it is two figures
- **Self-contained caption**: what, where, n, and the key reading ("Arrow marks the divergence point")
- **Legible at 100% print size** — fonts ≥ 7pt at final size; axis labels with units
- **Colorblind-safe palette** (≈8% of male readers); never encode meaning in color alone
- **Tables for exact values, figures for trends** — not both for the same data
- **Reference every item from the text** ("Fig. 2") — an unreferenced figure is dead weight
- **Rights and data availability** for reused items

**Production**: matplotlib/ggplot for generation (see visualization skill for the craft
standards); Overleaf for sizing/placement to the venue's column width.

## Session protocol

1. **Watch** the paired Ref-n-Write video (5–10 minutes).
2. **Read** this guide's working rules once.
3. **Draft or revise** your section applying one rule at a time — not all at once.
4. **Run** `skills/04_data_intelligence/visualization_master.md` on the result.
5. **Score** with `evaluators/CLARITY_ANALYZER.md` (target ≥80) and, where the section carries
   empirical claims, `evaluators/RIGOR_RUBRIC.md`.
