## What this section must accomplish

The abstract is a complete mini-paper: background, gap, methods, key results, and implication,
in roughly 150–300 words depending on venue. It is written *last* and read *first* — most
rejection decisions begin here. Nature's summary paragraph cap is 200 words; engineering venues
often allow more. The Ref-n-Write video walks the same arc in five minutes; Wiley gives the
abstract five full lessons — the most of any section in their course.

## Working rules

**The five-move abstract**
1. **Context** — one sentence establishing the field and why it matters
2. **Gap** — one sentence on what was unknown or unsolved
3. **Methods** — one to two sentences: approach, data, scale
4. **Results** — two to three sentences with actual numbers (the finding, not "results are discussed")
5. **Implication** — one sentence on what changes now

**Rules that survive every venue**: no citations; no abbreviations undefined; numbers over
adjectives ("23% faster", not "significantly faster"); and it must stand alone — no "as shown
in Section 3". Verify the venue's word cap against the ARES template before trimming.

## Session protocol

1. **Watch** the paired Ref-n-Write video (5–10 minutes).
2. **Read** this guide's working rules once.
3. **Draft or revise** your section applying one rule at a time — not all at once.
4. **Run** `skills/05_writing_excellence/abstract_mastery.md` on the result.
5. **Score** with `evaluators/CLARITY_ANALYZER.md` (target ≥80) and, where the section carries
   empirical claims, `evaluators/RIGOR_RUBRIC.md`.
