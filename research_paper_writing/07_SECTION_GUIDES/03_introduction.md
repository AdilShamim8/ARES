## What this section must accomplish

The introduction is a funnel: broad context → specific problem → the gap → your contribution
→ roadmap. The classic scholarly model is Swales' CARS (Create A Research Space): establishing
the territory, establishing the niche (the gap), and occupying the niche (your work) — the
theory taught in Swales & Feak and used, implicitly, by every modern guide including the
Ref-n-Write video. A reviewer decides within the introduction whether the paper claims
something new; the gap sentence is where that claim lives.

## Working rules

**The funnel, sentence by sentence**
1. **Territory** (1–3 sentences): the field and its stakes — cite reviews, not textbooks
2. **Niche** (2–4 sentences): what specifically remains unsolved — this is the *gap statement*, and ARES's `01_ideation/gap_analysis_prompt.md` exists to forge it
3. **Occupation** (3–5 sentences): your question, approach, and what you found — stated plainly, with the contribution named
4. **Roadmap** (0–1 sentences): "Section 2 reviews…; Section 3 presents…" — some venues consider it filler; check recent issues

**Common failure**: introductions that are 80% literature summary and 20% contribution —
the exact imbalance the adversarial reviewer in `06_quality_fortress/` attacks first.

## Session protocol

1. **Watch** the paired Ref-n-Write video (5–10 minutes).
2. **Read** this guide's working rules once.
3. **Draft or revise** your section applying one rule at a time — not all at once.
4. **Run** `skills/05_writing_excellence/introduction_craft.md` on the result.
5. **Score** with `evaluators/CLARITY_ANALYZER.md` (target ≥80) and, where the section carries
   empirical claims, `evaluators/RIGOR_RUBRIC.md`.
