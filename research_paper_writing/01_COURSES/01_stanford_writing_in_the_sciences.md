## What it covers (8 units)

1. **Principles of effective writing** — cutting clutter, writing clearly and concisely
2. **Verbs** — active vs. passive, strong active verbs, reuniting subjects and predicates, grammar
3. **Sentence and paragraph** — punctuation (colons, dashes, parentheses, semicolons), parallelism, focused paragraphs
4. **The writing process** — pre-writing, drafting, substantive revision
5. **The original manuscript** — drafting each paper section, critiquing tables and figures, strong Results and Introductions, Discussion content
6. **Scientific publishing** — plagiarism, peer review, authorship criteria, predatory journals
7. **Beyond the research manuscript** — review articles, grants, letters of recommendation, personal essays
8. **Communication with broader audiences** — media interviews, science news structure

## Why it is recommended here

This is the single most-cited free scientific-writing course in the world and the default first
recommendation across university lists and community threads (Class Central, MoocList, and
multiple university LibGuides point to it). Its distinctive move is starting at the *sentence*
level — clutter, verbs, parallelism — before touching manuscript structure, which is the
foundation the rest of this library builds on.
